# Getting started and building habits

## The five-minute start
Procrastination is often about avoiding an uncomfortable feeling, not laziness. Commit only to five minutes of the task. Starting is the hardest part, and momentum usually carries you further.

## Implementation intentions
Plans work better as "if-then" sentences: "When I finish lunch, I will open my thesis document and write one paragraph." Linking a new habit to an existing routine gives it a cue.

## Make it small and visible
Shrink a habit until it is almost too easy, then grow it. Keep the cue visible (a book on the pillow, running shoes by the door) and track it simply, for example a mark on a calendar.

## Work in focused blocks
Short focused blocks, such as 25 minutes of work and a 5-minute break, make large tasks feel manageable and reduce the urge to switch to distractions.
