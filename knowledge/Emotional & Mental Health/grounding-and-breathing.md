# Grounding and breathing for anxious moments

When anxiety spikes, the body reacts as if there is a threat even when there is none. Two simple skills help bring attention back to the present.

## 5-4-3-2-1 grounding
Name five things you can see, four you can hear, three you can touch, two you can smell and one you can taste. Saying them quietly or in your head shifts attention from racing thoughts to the senses. It works anywhere, including in meetings or on public transport.

## Box breathing
Breathe in for a count of four, hold for four, breathe out for four, hold for four. Repeat for one to three minutes. A slightly longer out-breath than in-breath (for example in for four, out for six) tends to calm the body further.

## When to get more help
If anxiety or panic happens most days, stops you doing things you need to do, or comes with thoughts of harming yourself, talk to a doctor or a licensed mental health professional.
