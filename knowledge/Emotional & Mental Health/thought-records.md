# Checking unhelpful thoughts

Cognitive behavioural approaches teach that how we interpret a situation shapes how we feel. A thought record is a short way to slow that process down.

1. Write the situation in one line: what happened, where, with whom.
2. Write the automatic thought, for example "I always mess things up."
3. Rate how strongly you believe it, from 0 to 100.
4. List the evidence for and against the thought, as if you were a fair friend.
5. Write a more balanced thought, for example "I got this one thing wrong, and I have handled harder things before."
6. Re-rate your belief in the original thought.

Common thinking traps to look for: all-or-nothing thinking, mind reading, catastrophising, and labelling yourself from a single event. The aim is not forced positivity, but a view that fits the facts better.
