# Structured problem solving

When a problem feels too big, breaking it down makes action possible.

1. Define the problem in one specific sentence.
2. Brainstorm every possible option without judging them yet.
3. For the three most realistic options, list pros and cons.
4. Pick one and plan the first concrete step: what, when and where.
5. Do the step, then review what happened and adjust.

Separating "thinking about options" from "deciding" stops the loop of worrying without acting. Writing the steps down also makes it easier to ask someone else for help with a specific part.
